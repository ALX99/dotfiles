# Hillclimb

1. Define the workload, metric, direction, completion condition, and correctness, error, and resource guardrails. Confirm the harness measures the intended work and identify its limiter; record the baseline and noise.
2. Form a mechanism-based hypothesis and change one thing per iteration. Compare repeated measurements under matched conditions; log hypothesis, result, and decision.
3. Keep gains that exceed noise without violating guardrails or adding unjustified complexity. Check representative inputs beyond the tuned case. Discard only your own failed experiments safely.
4. Change tactics on a plateau and obey stop conditions. Report baseline, final result, methodology, attempts, and uncertainty. Use an applicable autoresearch skill when available.
